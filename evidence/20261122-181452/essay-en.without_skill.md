For the first three days, the rented room felt empty. I had moved to a new city for university, but the room seemed to belong to no one. Its bare walls and floor offered nothing to hold on to.

My father had packed an old desk fan in a cardboard box. He had repaired it before I left. He said nothing about money. He only explained how to change the fuse if the motor ran hot.

I left the box alone at first. Then, on the third afternoon, I opened it and set the fan in the room. I plugged it in.

The blades began to turn. Nothing else changed: the room was still rented, still bare, and I was still new to the city. But the fan’s steady movement filled the silence with something familiar. I watched it for a while, with my father’s small instruction in mind.

It was the first time the room felt like home.
