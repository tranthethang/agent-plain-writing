# A 20 kg alert stopped our flour runouts

Last month, I replaced our paper order slips with a shared online sheet and automatic low-stock alerts. The first alert came too late, and we ran out of flour again.

## Paper slips hid the shortage

Paper slips hid what was running low until the shelf was empty. Before the switch, we ran out of flour twice in three weeks. In those same three weeks, we threw away 18 kg of unsold dough.

I also made two emergency trips to a wholesale store. Each trip took 40 minutes.

## My first alert came too late

I first set the alert at 10 kg. During the first week, flour ran out once more after the alert.

Our supplier needs two days to deliver, and we use about 8 kg of flour each day. At 10 kg, the alert did not give us enough time to wait for a delivery.

## One shared number changed the routine

I moved the alert to 20 kg. In the next four weeks, we did not run out of flour. Waste was 4 kg per week after the change.

The sheet gave the three staff members who update it one shared place to check the stock. It also gave us a fixed number to use when deciding when to reorder.

## A reminder kept the sheet current

In the first week, staff forgot to update the sheet twice. I printed a reminder and taped it next to the till.

Checking the sheet takes five minutes at closing time. That short check helps us keep the stock information current.

If you track stock on paper, try one shared sheet, set a reorder number that allows for your supplier’s delivery time, and check it at closing.
