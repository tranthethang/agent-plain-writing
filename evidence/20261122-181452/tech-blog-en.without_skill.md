# A shared sheet stopped our flour surprises

I used to count flour bags by hand and keep orders on paper slips. Twice in three weeks, we ran out of flour. We also threw away 18 kg of unsold dough in those three weeks, and I made two emergency trips to a wholesale store. Each trip took 40 minutes.

Last month, we switched to a free online spreadsheet shared by the three staff members who update our stock. It sends an alert when flour gets low. The sheet is simple: we record what’s on hand, and the alert reminds us when it’s time to reorder.

## The first alert came too late

I first set the alert at 10 kg. It went off on a Thursday evening, but the flour ran out on Saturday morning.

That wasn’t enough time. Our supplier takes two days to deliver, and we use about 8 kg of flour a day. So by the time the delivery arrived, we could use around 16 kg.

I moved the alert to 20 kg. That gives us time to order before we’re scraping the shelf. In the four weeks after I made the change, we had no flour stockouts. Waste was 4 kg per week.

## How we keep the sheet useful

- **We use one shared sheet.** Paper slips can hide what’s running low until the shelf is empty. With one shared place to check, everyone can see the stock.
- **We set a fixed reorder number.** For flour, that’s 20 kg. You’ll need to choose a number that allows for how quickly you use an item and how long delivery takes.
- **We check it for five minutes at closing time.** That’s enough to update the sheet and see what needs ordering.
- **We remind everyone to update it.** In the first week, staff forgot twice. I printed a reminder and taped it next to the till.

The sheet isn’t complicated, but it only helps if we keep it up to date. A quick check at closing is easier than another emergency flour run.
