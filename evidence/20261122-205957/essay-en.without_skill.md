For the first three days, the rented room looked like a place I was passing through. I had moved to a new city for university, but the room held little to suggest that I belonged there. Its emptiness seemed to settle around the things I had brought.

My father had packed an old desk fan in a cardboard box. He had repaired it before I left. He said nothing about money. He only explained how to change the fuse if the motor ran hot.

The box stayed in the room while I adjusted to being there. On the third afternoon, I took out the fan and plugged it in. I watched it turn on.

Nothing else in the room changed. It was still rented, still bare. But the fan was running, and it was something my father had fixed and sent with me. Until then, I had been sleeping in a room. In that moment, it began to feel like a place I could live. A place that, for now, was home.
