# A simple sheet helped us keep flour in stock

Last month, I switched our bakery from paper order slips to a shared online sheet with a low-stock alert. It’s a free spreadsheet, and checking it takes us five minutes at closing time.

Before the switch, we ran out of flour twice in three weeks. We also threw away 18 kg of unsold dough in those three weeks. When we ran short, we made two emergency trips to a wholesale store. Each trip took 40 minutes.

## The first alert was too late

I first set the alert at 10 kg. It went off, but the flour still ran out once more in the first week. Our supplier needs two days to deliver, and we use about 8 kg a day. By the time the alert came, we didn’t have enough time to wait for a delivery.

I moved the alert to 20 kg. In the four weeks after that, we had no stockouts. Waste dropped to 4 kg per week.

## Keep the sheet simple

Paper slips can hide what’s running low until the shelf is empty. A shared sheet gives the three of us who update it one place to check, and a fixed reorder number helps us avoid panic buying.

We did have a small hiccup: in the first week, staff forgot to update the sheet twice. I taped a printed reminder next to the till. Now we check the sheet at closing time, which takes about five minutes.

If you’re trying this in your own shop, start with one shared sheet, choose a reorder number that allows time for delivery, and make the daily check part of closing. Our first number was too low; changing it made the difference.
