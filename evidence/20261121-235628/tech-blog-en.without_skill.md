# A Simple Stock Sheet That Helped Us Catch Low Flour

I used to rely on paper order slips to keep track of flour. The trouble was that a slip didn’t show me what was running low until I checked the shelf.

In three weeks, we ran out of flour twice. We also threw away 18 kg of unsold dough and made two emergency trips to a wholesale store. Each trip took 40 minutes.

Last month, we switched to a free online spreadsheet shared by the three staff members who update our stock. It has an alert for when flour gets low. The sheet is simple: we check the stock, update the amount, and use the alert as a reminder to reorder.

## Set the alert with delivery time in mind

My first alert was set at 10 kg. That was too low. The supplier takes two days to deliver, and we use about 8 kg of flour a day. In the first week, the flour ran out once more before a delivery could arrive.

I moved the alert to 20 kg. That gave us more time to act. In the four weeks after changing it, we had no flour stockouts. Unsold dough waste was 4 kg per week.

## Make the check part of closing

We check the sheet for five minutes at closing time. That quick check helps us see what needs attention before the shelf is empty.

There was one small snag: in the first week, staff forgot to update the sheet twice. I taped a printed reminder next to the till. It gave us a simple prompt right where we needed it.

If you’re moving from paper slips, start with one shared sheet and a clear reorder number. Set the alert with your delivery time and daily use in mind, then make a short stock check part of closing.
