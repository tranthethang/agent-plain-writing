# A 20 kg alert gave us time to reorder flour

Last month, I replaced our paper order slips with a shared online sheet and low-stock alerts. The first alert came too late, so I changed the reorder point.

## Paper slips hid the warning

Before the switch, we ran out of flour twice in three weeks. We also threw away 18 kg of unsold dough during those same three weeks. Paper slips did not give us one shared view of what was running low, so we could miss the warning until the shelf was empty.

We made two emergency trips to a wholesale store for flour before the switch. Each trip took 40 minutes. I wanted a way to see the stock level and act before we ran out, rather than find out when we needed flour.

## The first alert came too late

I set the first alert at 10 kg. It went off, but the supplier needs two days to deliver, and we use about 8 kg of flour a day. In the first week, we ran out once more. The alert had warned us, but not early enough for the delivery time and our daily use.

That showed me that an alert number has to leave enough time to reorder. A warning is not useful just because it appears. It needs to arrive while there is still enough stock to cover the wait for a delivery.

## One shared sheet, one reorder point

I moved the alert to 20 kg in the shared sheet. The sheet is a free online spreadsheet, and three staff members update it. We use the same reorder point instead of relying on separate paper slips to track what is low.

After I moved the alert to 20 kg, we had no stockouts in the next four weeks. Waste also dropped to 4 kg per week. Those results followed the change, though they do not tell me that every bakery should use the same number. Our supplier takes two days, and our shop uses about 8 kg of flour a day. Another shop would need to set its alert around its own delivery time and use.

## Make the check part of closing

The sheet takes five minutes to check at closing time. That gives us a regular point to look at the stock and see whether the alert calls for a reorder. The sheet only helps if someone keeps it up to date.

In the first week, staff forgot to update it twice. I taped a printed reminder next to the till. That was the fix I used for the missed updates; the reminder put the task where staff would see it during the workday.

If you move from paper slips to a shared sheet, choose an alert point that accounts for both delivery time and daily use. Then make the stock check part of closing, and put a reminder where staff will see it.
