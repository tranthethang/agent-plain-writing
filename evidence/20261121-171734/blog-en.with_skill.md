# Set one flour alert before the shelf is empty

A small bakery ran out of flour twice in three weeks while tracking orders on paper slips. After switching to a shared online sheet with a 20 kg reorder alert, it wasted 4 kg of dough per week.

## Paper slips hide low stock

Paper slips can record what people order, but they don’t show the whole shop’s stock in one place. By the time someone sees that flour is running low, the shelf may already be empty.

That’s what happened at this bakery. Before the change, it also threw away 18 kg of unsold dough. I wouldn’t treat those numbers as proof that paper alone caused every problem. But they show how costly it can be when stock is hard to see and orders are handled one at a time.

## Put stock in one shared sheet

The bakery moved from paper slips to one shared online sheet. It added an alert for flour when stock reached 20 kg, giving the shop a clear point to reorder instead of waiting until flour ran out.

A fixed number matters because it gives everyone the same signal. You don’t have to guess whether the remaining flour will last, or place a rushed order after the shelf is empty. The sheet won’t make the decision for you, but it can make the low-stock point visible.

## Make the check part of closing

The daily check takes 5 minutes at closing time. That’s a small, defined task: look at the sheet, check stock, and respond to any alert.

I’d keep the check tied to a regular moment in the day. If it depends on someone remembering to look whenever they have time, it may be easy to miss. Closing time gives the check a place in the routine without adding a long stock-counting session.

## Set the number for your shop

After the bakery set the alert, waste dropped to 4 kg per week. That result belongs to this shop; it doesn’t tell you what alert number will work for yours.

Start with the item you most need to keep in stock, then choose a reorder point your team can use consistently. Put that number in the shared sheet and check it at closing.
