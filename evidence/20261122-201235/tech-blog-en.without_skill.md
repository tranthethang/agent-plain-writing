# How a Shared Sheet Helped Us Keep Flour in Stock

I used to rely on paper order slips to keep track of flour. The problem was that a slip didn’t show me what was running low until the shelf was nearly empty.

In three weeks, we ran out of flour twice. We also threw away 18 kg of unsold dough, and I made two emergency trips to a wholesale store. Each trip took 40 minutes.

Last month, we moved to a free online spreadsheet shared by the three staff members who update our stock. It sends an alert when flour reaches the reorder amount.

## The first alert was too low

I first set the alert at 10 kg. That was too late: our supplier takes two days to deliver, and we use about 8 kg of flour each day. We ran out once more in the first week.

So I changed the alert to 20 kg. That gives us more time to order before the flour runs out. In the four weeks after the change, we had no stockouts.

## Keep the check simple

At closing time, we check the sheet. It takes about five minutes. The important part is making sure someone updates it each day, so the alert reflects the stock we actually have.

We missed two updates in the first week. I taped a printed reminder next to the till, where staff would see it. That fixed the problem for us.

Since moving the alert to 20 kg, we’ve thrown away 4 kg of dough per week, compared with 18 kg over the three weeks before the switch.

If you’re still using paper slips, start with one shared sheet and a fixed reorder amount for the stock you can’t afford to run out of. Then check it at closing time and make the reminder hard to miss.
