# A 20 kg flour alert changed our stock routine

I used to manage flour orders with paper slips. They made it hard to see what was running low until the shelf was nearly empty. In three weeks, we ran out of flour twice and made two emergency trips to a wholesale store. Each trip took 40 minutes.

We also threw away 18 kg of unsold dough in those three weeks.

Last month, we switched to a free online spreadsheet shared by the three staff members who update our stock. It has an automatic low-stock alert. I thought setting the alert at 10 kg would be enough. It wasn’t: the alert came too late, and we ran out of flour once more in the first week.

Our supplier takes two days to deliver, and we use about 8 kg of flour a day. That’s about 16 kg while we wait for an order. I moved the alert to 20 kg instead.

In the four weeks after that change, we had no flour stockouts. Waste dropped to 4 kg per week.

## How we keep the sheet useful

- We use one shared sheet, so the people updating stock are looking at the same information.
- We keep a fixed reorder number: 20 kg for flour.
- We check the sheet for five minutes at closing time.

There was one snag. In the first week, staff forgot to update the sheet twice. I taped a printed reminder next to the till. It’s a simple prompt to update the stock as part of the daily routine.

If you’re still using paper slips, a shared sheet and a clear reorder number may be a practical place to start. The alert needs to account for how long your supplier takes and how much stock you use while you wait. Our first number, 10 kg, didn’t do that. At 20 kg, we had time to order without running out.
