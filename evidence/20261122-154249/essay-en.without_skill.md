For the first three days, the rented room felt empty. I had moved to a new city for university, but the room still seemed like a place I was only staying in.

My suitcase sat in the corner with its broken zipper tied shut by a piece of twine. Beside it was a cardboard box my father had packed. Inside was an old desk fan he had repaired.

He said nothing about money. He only explained how to change the fuse if the motor ran hot. I listened, then left the box unopened.

On the third afternoon, I took the fan out and plugged it in. The motor started, and the blades began to turn. I stayed where I was for a moment, listening to it.

The room did not change. It was still rented and bare. But with the fan running, it felt lived in for the first time. It felt like home.
